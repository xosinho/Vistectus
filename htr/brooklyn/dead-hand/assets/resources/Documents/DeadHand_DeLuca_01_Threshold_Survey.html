Survey of the Thin Places of New York, by Fra Matteo de Luca, custodian · 1887 – 1909

Depth is reckoned from I, where the veil is felt only by the instructed, to IX, which is the door itself. Character records how a place receives the one who measures it. No place in this city has yet been found deeper than VII.

Of the hollow at Degraw Street, 1903

The ground holds its cold out of every season. In August my breath clouded over the hollow and nowhere else in the room. A candle set above it leans toward the centre and stays leaning, though no air moves. When I spoke the office of measure, it attended. I have measured a hundred places that suffer the dead. This one listens.

It is older than the city, and older, I think, than any name we have for it. The people who walked here before the Dutch will have had one. We do not.

— M. de L.
